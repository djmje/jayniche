If you've fallen behind on your mortgage in Ontario, the word you're likely to hear is **power of sale**. It's how most Ontario lenders recover what they're owed, and it can move faster than people expect.

## Step 1: Missed payments

It starts with a missed payment. Most lenders will contact you and try to work something out. **Call your lender early.** You have the most options before formal steps begin.

## Step 2: The Notice of Sale

Most Ontario mortgages include a power of sale clause. Under the **Mortgages Act**, the lender can't send a **Notice of Sale under Mortgage** until the payment has been in default for at least **15 days**.

The notice goes to you and to anyone else with a registered interest in the property, such as a second mortgage holder.

## Step 3: The 35-day period

The lender can't sell the house until at least **35 days** after the notice is given. During this period you can usually still:

- Pay what's in arrears plus the lender's costs to put the mortgage back in good standing (check your mortgage and get legal advice)
- Refinance with another lender
- **Sell the house yourself** and pay off the mortgage from the proceeds

If the mortgage doesn't include a power of sale clause, the statutory version requires **three months** of default and **45 days'** notice.

## Step 4: The lender sells

After the notice period, the lender can take possession and sell the house. It pays itself the balance owing plus legal and sale costs. Any surplus goes to you (and other creditors in order of priority). If there's a shortfall, you may still owe the difference.

## Why selling yourself can help

When you sell before the lender does, you choose the buyer, the price and the closing date. Fewer enforcement costs get added to your balance, and you can plan your move.
