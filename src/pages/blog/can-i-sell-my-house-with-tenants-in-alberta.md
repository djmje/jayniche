Yes. You can sell a rental property in Alberta while tenants live there. What you can't do is end their tenancy just because you've decided to sell. Here's how it works.

## A sale doesn't end the tenancy

Under Alberta's **Residential Tenancies Act**, the lease carries on when the property changes hands. The buyer becomes the new landlord with the same lease.

If the tenant has a **fixed-term lease**, it generally runs until its end date. Unless you and the tenant agree otherwise, they can stay until then.

## When a sale lets you end a monthly tenancy

For a **monthly periodic tenancy**, a landlord can end it because of a sale in specific cases, once the sale is firm (all conditions satisfied or waived) **and the buyer asks in writing**:

- **The buyer or a relative will move in**, or
- **The property is a detached house, semi-detached house or single condo unit.** In this case the buyer doesn't have to move in.

Either way, the landlord must give **three months' written notice**. The notice must include the address, the date the tenancy ends, the reason, and the landlord's signature.

## Showings with tenants

Tenants have a right to proper notice before you enter. Showings can be hard to coordinate, and some tenants understandably aren't keen on strangers walking through their home.

## Selling with tenants in place

Many investors, including us, buy rental houses **with tenants in place**. That means:

- No notices to serve and no waiting on lease dates
- One viewing at most, with proper notice
- The lease simply transfers to us

If you're a tired landlord, have a tenant who isn't paying, or inherited a rental, see [selling a house with tenants in Alberta](/alberta/sell-house-with-tenants/) or our pages for [Calgary](/alberta/calgary/) and [Edmonton](/alberta/edmonton/).

Disputes between landlords and tenants in Alberta can go to the **Residential Tenancy Dispute Resolution Service (RTDRS)** or the courts. Check the current rules on Alberta.ca before serving any notice.
