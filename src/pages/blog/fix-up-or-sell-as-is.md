When a house needs a new roof, foundation work or a gut renovation, you have two choices: fix it and list it, or sell it as it is. Here's how to think it through.

## Questions to ask yourself

**1. Do you have the cash, or can you borrow it?** Big repairs usually need to be paid up front, before any sale money comes in.

**2. Do you have the time?** Contractors, permits and inspections take weeks to months, and the house keeps costing money (taxes, insurance, utilities) the whole time.

**3. Will the repair add more than it costs?** Some repairs, like fixing a leaking roof or a flooded basement, help a lot with buyers. Others, like a high-end kitchen in an older house, often return less than they cost.

**4. Can you manage it?** Supervising contractors is a job in itself, and harder if you live far away or are dealing with an estate or separation.

## The costs people forget

- Carrying costs during the renovation and the listing
- Surprises behind the walls (old wiring, mould, rot)
- Permit fees and inspection delays
- Agent commission on the higher sale price
- Repairs or credits the buyer asks for after their inspection

## When selling as-is makes sense

- The repairs are structural, or the house has [fire or water damage](/situations/house-needs-repairs/)
- You don't have the cash or time for a renovation
- The house is part of an estate ([Ontario](/ontario/inherited-house/), [Alberta](/alberta/inherited-house/))
- Buyers with mortgages are struggling to get financing or insurance on it

## Two ways to sell as-is

You can **list it as-is with a realtor**, which can work, but many buyers need a mortgage, and lenders can be cautious with homes that have serious issues. Or you can **sell direct to a cash buyer** who prices the repairs into the offer and closes without financing conditions.
